Xiph.org Names and Logos

Xiph

Xiphophorus helleri is a small aquarium fish (the common Swordtail). What's special about it? Not much, really. The Xiph.org logo doesn't even look anything like a real swordtail, but it's a logo that's been in use a long time.

What the name does have is the requisite X for a technology-related organization. That it's impossible to spell is an added bonus.

Ogg

An ogg is a tactical maneuver from Netrek that has entered into common usage in a wider sense. From the definition:

To do anything forcefully, possibly without consideration of the drain on future resources. "I guess I'd better go ogg the problem set that's due tomorrow." "Whoops! I looked down at the map for a sec and almost ogged that oncoming car."

Ogg became a project when most personal computers were i386s and the i486 was new. Some of the algorithms considered would require a 486; while the software ogged the music, there wasn't much processor left for anything else.

These days, Ogg refers to the multimedia container format; there wasn't a good reason to have "Ogg" refer to both a container format and a project.

Incidentally, Ogg is not named after Nanny Ogg, the character from the Terry Pratchett book Wyrd Sisters.

Vorbis

Unlike Ogg, Vorbis is named after the Terry Pratchett character from the book Small Gods. The name holds some significance, but it's an indirect, uninteresting story. Vorbis is the current general-purpose audio compression codec that was started after Fraunhofer issued its Letter of Infringement to free MP3 encoder efforts, and is intended to go head-to-head with AAC and MPC.

Theora

Theora is named after Theora Jones, Edison Carter's controller on the Max Headroom television series. Incidentally, she was played by Amanda Pays.

cdparanoia

cdparanoia is the error correcting compact disc digital audio extraction (CDDA DAE) tool currently built with Paranoia III.

The name should be somewhat self-explanatory to anyone who's had difficulty getting accurate audio from compact discs; the logo is a bit weirder. Dubbed The All-Seeing Laser Playback Head of Omniscience, it's a takeoff of the eye-and-pyramid symbol of wisdom. Think you've seen it before and can't quite place where? Look on the back of a US one dollar bill.
