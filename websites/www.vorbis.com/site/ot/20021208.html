1. A Message from Emmett Plant

Hey, kids! I don't usually take the time to write in Ogg Traffic, but I figured there was enough exciting things coming up (and a bit of an explanation to go into) that it warranted me stepping in this week. Please forgive my run-on sentences, I've been in all-out work mode this week.

First, you'll notice that there is a big red advertisement on the front page of vorbis.com. What's that all about? We haven't run ads in the past, why would we do so now? Well, the good folks at ashampoo pulled a big dump truck full of money to my front door, and asked if I would consider running their ads. What would you do?

I'm just kidding. The decision to start running ads on vorbis.com wasn't an easy one to make. We're not big on 'selling out our community,' and I try to make sure that we don't do that as time goes by. Ashampoo sends us updates once in a while on their software, and they make really nice software that uses our codecs. They asked about advertising, and I thought about it and asked some people in the community about it, and this is what I came up with.

Advertising on Vorbis.com would not be objectionable if:

  1. The product advertised actually had something to do with Ogg Vorbis.

  2. The advertisement was small and didn't stop anyone from getting to any information on the page.

  3. The advertisement was not animated.

I decided to add a ground rule of my own:

  1. The advertisement had to be presented in an open format like PNG.

So, the ad on the front page conforms to those standards, and the good folks at ashampoo didn't have a problem with it. They even gave us a choice of ads to choose from. They've been really nice to work with, and I hope you support my decision to run their ad. As always, any objections can be sent to my INBOX.

What else is new? Auratus, but I think I'll wait until next week to write about it. It's something that should address two of the most common questions I get, being 'Where can I find music in Vorbis' and 'MP3.com sucks, do you know of an alternative?' So, more news on that next week, it should be exciting.

Also, Theora Alpha Two should be coming out this week! Hooray!

That's it for me, I've been too long-winded already. Don't forget to stop by and chat with us on #vorbis on irc.xiph.org, we'd love to hear from you. Also, there's new stuff at the Gift Shop in case you missed the announcement last week. If there's stuff you'd like to see in the gift shop, let me know!

Excelsior!

3. Interesting Discussions

3.1. Very Low Bitrate Encoding

Christoph Leske asked in this message about the possibility of encoding Ogg Vorbis at very low bitrate, i.e. less than 45kbps.

This is indeed possible, but not with 44.1kHz stereo data, as the replies clarified. To achieve very low bitrates, the data must be resampled and/or downmixed to a lower sample rate and/or mono. This can be achieved on the fly in oggenc by using the --resample and --downmix parameters.

Michael Smith pointed out here that the need for resampling or downmixing to achieve low bitrates is not unique to Ogg Vorbis. The only difference is that Ogg Vorbis doesn't make default assumptions like other encoders, and requires the user to specify how they intend to achieve the low bitrate they are asking for.

3.2. YATT (Yet Another Transcoding Thread)

The recent discussion on transcoding and why it is generally a bad idea prompted a reader to ask if it's technically possible to transcode MP3 to Ogg Vorbis in a better, lossless way.

This is not a new question, and the answer is, no, it can not be done. The formats are too different, so it's impossible to just take MP3 frame data and somehow shoehorn it into Vorbis frame data. The only possibility is to fully decode the MP3 data, and then reencode it into Ogg Vorbis, which is of course the dreaded lossy transcoding process.

3.3. Ogg Vorbis in Games

Since Ogg Vorbis offers better compression than MP3 and doesn't require any licensing fees ever, Ogg Vorbis is a logical choice for providing in-game music. A recent discovery by an alert Vorbis fan adds James Bond 007: Nightfire to the list of games that use Ogg Vorbis:

  • Unreal Tournament 2003

  • Serious Sam: The Second Encounter

  • Operation Flashpoint

  • Nascar Racing games from Papyrus

  • Candy Cruncher

  • Shogun: Total War

4. Recent Developments

4.1. (A kind of) Hardware Ogg Vorbis player

Recently, two independent audio players for Palm OS have surfaced, and they both support Ogg Vorbis. The announcements can be found here and here.

This adds another member to the list of platforms that support Ogg Vorbis, but it's probably not what people mean when they clamor for Ogg Vorbis hardware players. If you would like to see Ogg Vorbis on hardware players, please go to the hardware page and let the hardware manufacturers know that there is a demand that needs to be filled.
