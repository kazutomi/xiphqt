1. Status Updates

1.1. Monty

Monty committed the Vorbis modes for low sample rate (8, 11, 16, and 22 kHz) and retuned the 44/48 kHz modes slightly. He also corrected some problems with the way bitrate management options were handled in oggenc. Monty has been spending much of the week writing the Vorbis specification.

1.2. Michael Smith

Michael made some last minute fixes to some of the vorbis tools before leaving on vacation this week. He correct some UTF-8 handling bugs in ogginfo and updated oggenc to support the new negative quality modes in the Vorbis encoder. The quality can now be sent between -1.0 and 10.0. A quality -1 file tends to be around 45-50 kbps for stereo 44.1 kHz audio.

1.3. Stan Seibert

This week Stan made some build fixes to vorbis tools and fixed some of the library version information for libvorbis, libvorbisenc and libvorbisfile. A bug in the HTTP buffering used by ogg123 was corrected, preventing ogg123 from using 100% of the CPU when playing some URLs. Stan also added a --audio-buffer=x option (x is in kB) to allow users to control the size of the audio buffer in ogg123. Setting this to a value larger than 128 may prevent skipping on slower systems.

1.4. Chris Cheney

Chris updated the Debian packaging files and split the vorbis library package into three packages, one for libvorbis, libvorbisfile and libvorbisenc. This was necessary because the version numbers of these three libraries will diverge in the upcoming release. Chris also provided valuable assistance to Stan in figuring out the library version situation for the vorbis library.

1.5. Ralph Giles

Ralph updated the Mac project files and fixed some build bugs. He also updated ogginfo for Michael to be able to identify the version number for CVS encoded files and to not flag some CVS encoded files as having erroneous headers.

1.6. Emmett Plant

Emmett, the Xiph.org CEO, has been coordinating the activities of the various developers and build people. When he's not keeping tabs of the master TODO list, he's drawing diagrams for Monty that will be included in the final Vorbis specification.

1.7. Manuel Lora

Manuel, the Xiph.org webmaster, has been hard at work this getting web pages ready for the next release. He's been helping Monty prepare listening samples that will show what the new encoder can do. He's also been updating the documentation on the website.

2. Interesting Discussions

This week, we feature a new section in Ogg Traffic that will include summaries of the discussion threads and other postings on the Ogg-related mailing lists. I won't promise to cover every thread, but just those I think will be of general interest.

2.1. RTP Based Unicast and Multicast Vorbis

Peter Maersk-Moller announced preliminary code at http://sourceforge.net/forum/forum.php?thread_id=703395&forum_id=59136 that does RTP streaming of Vorbis packets in unicast and multicast modes.

2.2. Quality Comparison Between Vorbis Encoder Releases

There was a long conversation about whether the latest encoder in CVS achieves the same quality as previous encoders. Zvezdan Dimitrijevic contended that the beta 4 encoder was better than the CVS encoder. Monty disagreed, and posted a long reply explaining why. Zvezdan maintained his position, and later in the conversation produced graphs supporting his observation that less phase information was preserved in CVS as compared to beta 4. Monty agreed that more phase information was being removed in the encoders since RC2, but said that the information should be inaudible. He cautioned against looking for artifacts using graphs since the real test is whether you can actually hear the defects. Zvezdan replied that he was hearing a difference, and only used the graphs to try and identify what he was hearing. Monty pointed out that the graphs didn't conclusively prove that a lack of phase information was the source of the artifact. However, Zvezdan reported that when he increased the cutoff frequency for phase information to 16 kHz in the RC3 encoder, he was no longer able to hear the defect.

2.3. Uncoupled Modes

Pádár Atila asked how to force uncoupled encoding at high bitrates using the CVS encoder. It was pointed out that at the high bitrates mentioned, the channel coupling in Vorbis is lossless. Uncoupled encoding would preserve no extra information and take more space. Pádár went ahead and posted a modified version of the encoder that allowed completely uncoupled stereo. Monty replied that the feature would not be added to the Xiph.org encoder in the near future.

2.4. Vote for Vorbis!

Adrien Beau reported that the Linux Journal had opened voting for their 2002 Reader's Choice poll and that both the "Vorbis Tools" and ogg123 were listed as options in the Audio Tools category. Adrien observed that this was a little confusing since ogg123 is part of the Vorbis Tools package. Everyone interested in voting should do so before August 12, 2002.
