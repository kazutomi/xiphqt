Previous Issues of Ogg Traffic

1. Status Updates

1.1. Monty

Monty's main current focus within Ogg is on integration of projects; further tuning and improvement of the specific codecs is on hold until the larger framework is in place to glue the pieces together via a native mux/demux framework. The intent of this integration work is to allow transparent mix-and-match of different Ogg codecs for audio and video (and in the future, other media types such as arbitrary metadata); In short, to provide the necessary, coherent API to all Ogg components ala Quicktime or DirectShow (while simultaneously simplifying deployment of Ogg within these and other systems).

1.2. Michael Smith

Michael Smith applied lots of miscellaneous bugfixes and improvements to Icecast 2. Thanks to Karl Heyes, who contributed many patches, including better networking code.

1.3. Jean-Marc Valin

Jean-Marc Valin is making great progress with Speex. Recent additions to Speex beta 3 include support for ultra-wideband signals (sampling rates above 16kHz), intensity stereo, and a 50% to 100% speed improvement.

2. Interesting Discussions

2.1. Ogg Vorbis Industry Standard?

Peter Maersk-Moller shared the problems he has had convincing government agencies and businesses to adopt Ogg Vorbis as an alternative to MP3. The main problem is that, albeit well documented in technical documentation as well as source code, Ogg Vorbis is not a recognized standard.

As a possible solution, Linus Walleij pointed out Silvia Pfeiffer's work on creating an RFC draft for the Ogg encapsulation format. Silvia hopes to get the draft ready for the next IETF meeting. We'll let you know what happens.

2.2. Ogg Vorbis to MP3 transcoding

Once again, the issue of transcoding between Ogg Vorbis and MP3 has come up. This time, however, the question was not about going from MP3 to Ogg Vorbis, but from Ogg Vorbis to MP3, for the specific purpose of getting a PC based Ogg Vorbis collection into a portable player.

It was pointed out that transcoding is in general not a good idea, but it is a solution with an acceptable space gain versus quality loss tradeoff if you're only doing it in order to play the resulting files on a portable player.

3. Recent (and not so recent) Developments

3.1. Tremor

Since the open source release of Tremor, the community has contributed quite a few improvements in order to increase performance, reduce the memory footprint, and build the code on different platforms. Some examples are:

  • Nicolas Pitre, the author of a GPL'ed integerized version of the Vorbis decoder, contributed many ideas to the Tremor codebase. Among many other improvements, he reduced the memory footprint by consolidating the mathematical lookup tables, and he carefully tweaked and optimized the computationally most intensive parts in order to improve performance, especially on the ARM platform.

  • Andrew Leadbetter reported that he has managed to compile Tremor on Symbian OS v7.0. The port plays 44.1kHz stereo files in realtime on a Sony Ericsson P800.

3.2. Speex joins Xiph.org

Jean-Marc Valin, author of the patent free, open source speech codec Speex, joined Xiph.org, and re-released Speex under Xiph's open source license. Speex is a codec specifically designed for speech, which makes it an ideal codec for integration into Internet Telephony and VoIP projects. Speex Beta 3 was released on November 11, 2002.

3.3. Theora Alpha One

Ogg Theora, Xiph.org's project for integrating On2's VP3 video codec into the Ogg framework has released its first alpha on September 25, 2002. This release implements a simple, generic encode and decode API, a commandline Theora/Vorbis with full and correct muxing, and an example SDL player.

Keep your eyes peeled for alpha 2, which is scheduled to be released on December 1, 2002.

3.4. Icecast 2

Michael Smith has taken over the Icecast project. Icecast 2 is a streaming server that supports Ogg Vorbis and MP3 streaming, and is scalable to thousands of users. The first alpha of Icecast 2, released on October 10, 2002, is already very stable and ready for widespread use. It can be downloaded here.
