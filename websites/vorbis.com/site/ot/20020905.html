2. Interesting Discussions

2.1. Debian MP3->Vorbis Transcoding

Craig Dickson began a long thread discussing the possibility of Debian including an MP3 to Vorbis transcoder. He was concerned about the quality issues and asked other Debian developers on the vorbis list to take a look at the in-progress thread on the debian-devel list. David Parker noted that such a tool would have the same patent problems that other mp3 decoders would have. I summarized the reasons why you should and should not transcode and suggested that we pick an unofficial tag that should be used to mark transcoded files. Segher replied that a more general construct for marking the file's history would be better. He suggested a "FILE_HISTORY" tag which would have a freeform string describing the operations that had been performed on the file, including oggenc parameters and descriptions of previous encodings (ex: "FILE_HISTORY=192 kbps MP3"). A file could have several "FILE_HISTORY" tags if several operations had been performed on it (peeling, channel merging, etc.).

There was also some discussion of the legal status of MP3 decoders in light of the removal of the "non-commercial software doesn't have to pay royalties" wording from the MP3 licensing page. Moritz Grimm said that Thomson's position had not changed, and non-commercial software was still exempt from royalties. He did note, as did Greg Wooledge, that there was still a problem with distributing MP3 software under the GPL because the GPL required commercial redistribution to be permitted, but that was not allowed by the MP3 patent licensing rules without royalty payments.

2.2. Analog Artifact Estimation

John Bradford asked whether it would make sense for a video codec (like VP3) to include algorithms to deal with various sorts of common analog video artifacts, since a lot of video comes from analog sources. His post includes a very extensive list of common analog artifacts. Henry Mason said that some of the issues John brought up could be dealt with by preprocessing the video before giving it to the encoder stage. Daniel Miller (from On2) mentioned that, while John's points were interesting, the Theora project would be mostly focused on integrating VP3 into the Ogg framework and less so on improving the quality of the encoder. He did say that, just as in Vorbis, the VP3 format was flexible enough to permit the encoder to be improved while keeping the bitstream format fixed (and therefore all of the deployed decoders happy). So the ability to do significant tuning would be available in the future.

The thread got people thinking about various psychovisual issues (much as Vorbis uses psychoacoustic techniques to select audio components to discard) involved in compressing video. Daniel Miller talked about his own research into alternative metrics to use for estimating the quality of a video codec. He said that most codecs (including VP3) are designed with a great deal of weight given to the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), which he feels is inadequate to describe perceived quality.

3. New Developments

3.1. Xiph.org Releases Integerized Vorbis Decoder

Xiph.org decided on Sept. 2 to help speed the development of portable Vorbis players by releasing their integerized Vorbis decoder, named "Tremor," under a BSD-like license. Tremor is a Vorbis decoding library written for CPUs without floating point hardware, like most handheld devices use. It was previously a proprietary library--licensed by theKompany for their Sharp Zaurus player, among others--but now it's available for everyone to use.

Xiph.org is asking for help from end users to get the word out that it's time for hardware makers to start looking at Vorbis. The release page gives contact information for the manufacturers of many popular portable music players. We would ask that you politely contact the manufacturers of devices that you would like to see support Vorbis in the future. Chances are by now that they've heard about the Tremor release, but demonstrating that there is a large Vorbis user base will go a long way to convincing manufacturers that Vorbis support translates to increased sales.
