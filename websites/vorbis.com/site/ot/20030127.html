2. Interesting Discussions

2.1. Using Signal Difference for Quality Evaluation?

This message raises a somewhat frequently asked question about audio compression: Can the quality of an audio compression scheme be judged by listening to the sound that results from subtracting the original signal from the compressed signal?

The answer is, no, not for lossy compression. For lossless compression, the difference should be 0, of course, but for lossy compression, all bets are off. A compression method that produces a quieter difference is not necessarily better sounding that one that produces a louder difference. The reason is that lossy compression methods use psychoacoustic methods to discard information that the human ear can't hear in the context of the original signal. This creates a difference that's audible when the original signal is subtracted out, but that difference is not necessarily audible when played together with the original signal.

This phenomenon can be illustrated with the analogy of searching for a needle in a haystack. When you take away the haystack, the needle is immediately obvious, but inside the haystack, the needle is very hard to find.

3. Recent Developments

3.1. LinuxWorld Conference and Expo

The LinuxWorld Conference and Expo went well, and everybody has returned home safely. Emmett promised me a little write-up that should be available next week.

3.2. Icecast.org's New Face

Nathan has finished the icecast.org redesign, and the result is now accessible for all to see at icecast.org. Check it out!

3.3. Vorbis 1.0 GT3

Garf has spent some time tuning the higher bitrate modes of the Vorbis encoder to address the concerns of audiophiles that still prefer MPC over Vorbis at high bitrates. The first beta of Garf's tuned oggenc is available here and features tuning for quality levels 5 and higher. If you have good ears, please give it a try and tell Garf how it works for you.

3.4. Is that a Portable Vorbis Player on the Horizon?

It just might be! Xiph.org has entered into a preliminary agreement to bring Vorbis playback to the Neuros Digital Audio Computer. You can read the full announcement on the hardware page. Please note that this is not a guarantee that Vorbis will definitely work on these devices. We're working on it, but we won't make any promises we can't keep. Once we're sure that it'll work, we'll let you know.
