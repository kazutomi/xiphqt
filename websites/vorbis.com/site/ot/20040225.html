2   News and Recent Developments

2.1   Ogg granulepos discussion

To extend or not to extend, and how, was the question raised over discontinuous bitstreams in Ogg. When Ogg was initially designed only continuous bitstreams, whereas the last granule of each page is one less than the beginning granule of the next, were foreseen. This covers virtually all audio and video codecs.

The end-time was chosen so that the granulepos of the last page of a stream represented the stream's total length. Even if the stream is incomplete a decoder can use the granulepos of the last complete page. Granulepos is also used for streaming, seeking, and muxing (combining multiple codecs into an Ogg file so that they play at the same time).

However, not all codecs are continuous. Subtitle formats, for example, may have a long period of time between text phrases and the text phrases may overlap in duration. While the discontinuous nature of these codecs have been handled in various ways, such as OggMIDI using standard end-time granulepos but ordering by start-time, no standard has been found for these codecs.

One proposed solution for discontinuous bitstreams was to have the granulepos represent the start-time of a page. As demonstrated in the OggWrit spec, this results in discontinuous codecs appearing before continuous codecs in a muxed stream, but since most discontinuous codecs are fairly low bitrate this is unlikely to cause many buffering issues.

Another proposed solution, which is being favored at the moment, is for all codecs (except for Vorbis I, for backwards compatibility) to use start-time for granulepos. This has the added benefit of better buffering, but also eliminates the easy length discovery as described above.

This is a continuing discussion which may last several more weeks. Settling on a solution will open Ogg to an array of different codecs and move us closer to having a complete multimedia framework.

2.2   Icecast gets multi-codec Ogg support

Through two independent implementations, one by Karl Heyes and another by Daniel Holth, pre-encoded Ogg Theora files can now be streamed with Icecast2. Theora isn't alone, either, as Karl's code also adds Speex support while Daniel's adds FLAC support.

This is exciting news as, for the first time, royalty-free video streaming is possible. We may be just months away from opening the market to small web-based television stations which could not afford streaming licenses or per-viewer royalties previously imposed on them.

There are still bugs to work out, however, and this experimental work is not yet available to the general public. Currently both implementations pace the stream by Vorbis alone resulting in less than optimal buffering, wasted bandwidth on connect, and a requirement that Theora streams include Vorbis audio. Also, with both MPlayer and Xine you can currently only watch the first Ogg Theora file in a stream as neither is capable of playing multiple chained streams. These bugs are likely to be worked out in the upcoming weeks as we near an Icecast public release with debugged multi-codec streaming.
